## Definition

Crude oil is priced per **barrel** (about 159 litres), usually in US dollars. Because oil from different places has different qualities, the market relies on **benchmarks**:
- **Brent**: based on crude from the North Sea, used as the reference for much of the world's oil;
- **WTI** (West Texas Intermediate): the main US benchmark, delivered at Cushing, Oklahoma.

Prices depend on supply (including decisions by **OPEC+**, the group of oil producers led by Saudi Arabia and Russia), on demand from the global economy, and on geopolitical events.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If Brent rises from $70 to $90 a barrel, petrol, heating and transport become more expensive, and inflation figures in the following months are likely to be higher.

## Why it matters

- Oil prices affect inflation, consumer spending and company costs almost everywhere.
- Big price swings can shift wealth between oil-exporting and oil-importing countries.
- Energy shares and the currencies of oil exporters tend to move with the oil price.
