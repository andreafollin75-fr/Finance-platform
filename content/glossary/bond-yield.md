## Definition

A bond's yield is the return an investor can expect from it, expressed as a percentage per year. The simplest version, the **current yield**, is the annual coupon divided by the bond's market price. Professionals usually quote the **yield to maturity**, which also takes into account the gain or loss between today's price and the amount repaid at maturity.

The key rule: **when a bond's price goes up, its yield goes down, and vice versa.** The coupon is fixed, so paying more for the same coupon means a lower return.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A bond pays a €40 coupon every year and will repay €1,000. If its price falls from €1,000 to €950, a buyer still receives €40 a year but pays less for it, so the yield rises above 4%.

## Why it matters

- Bond yields are market interest rates: they show what it costs governments and companies to borrow today.
- Rising yields make borrowing more expensive throughout the economy and can weigh on stock prices.
- Yields reflect what investors expect for inflation and for central bank policy.
