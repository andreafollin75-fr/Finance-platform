## Definition

The Bank of England is the central bank of the United Kingdom. Its **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** sets the UK's main interest rate, called **Bank Rate**, with the goal of meeting the inflation target set by the government: 2%, measured by the consumer price index.

The MPC normally meets eight times a year. Its nine members vote, and the vote split is published, so markets can see how divided the committee is.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A "7–2" vote to hold rates means seven members wanted to leave Bank Rate unchanged and two wanted a change. Markets read such splits as hints about the next decision.

## Why it matters

- Bank Rate affects UK mortgages, savings and the value of the pound (sterling).
- The Bank of England also regulates UK banks and insurers and watches over financial stability.
