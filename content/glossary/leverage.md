## Definition

Leverage means using debt (or financial contracts such as derivatives) to control a larger position than your own money alone would allow.

It is often measured as a ratio: for a company, debt compared with its equity or its earnings; for an investor, the size of the position compared with their own capital.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* You invest €10,000 of your own money plus €40,000 borrowed, so you control €50,000 of assets (leverage of 5 to 1).
- If the assets rise 10%, you gain €5,000: 50% on your own money (before interest).
- If they fall 10%, you lose €5,000: half of your own money.
- If they fall 20%, your €10,000 is gone.

## Why it matters

- Leverage can boost returns, but it can wipe out investors quickly when prices move the wrong way.
- Highly leveraged investors may be forced to sell when prices fall (a **margin call**), which pushes prices down further.
- Excessive leverage in banks and households was a central cause of the 2008 financial crisis.
