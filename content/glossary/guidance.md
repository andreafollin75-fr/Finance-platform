## Definition

Guidance is what a company tells investors about the results it expects in a coming period, for example "we expect full-year revenue between $10 billion and $10.5 billion". Companies **raise** guidance when business is better than expected and **cut** (or "lower") it when it is worse.

Central banks use a related idea, **forward guidance**: communicating how they expect to set interest rates in the future.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A company reports good results for the last quarter but cuts its guidance for the rest of the year. Its share price may still fall, because investors care more about future profits than past ones.

## Why it matters

- Guidance often moves a share price more than the results themselves.
- It is a forecast, not a promise: companies can and do miss their own guidance.
- Some companies give no guidance at all, which makes analysts' estimates more uncertain.
