## Definition

A share (also called a stock, or equity) is a small piece of ownership in a company. Shareholders usually have the right to vote at the company's general meetings and to receive part of the profits if the company pays dividends.

Shares of **listed** companies are traded on stock exchanges, such as the New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq or Euronext, where their price changes constantly with supply and demand.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A company is divided into 1,000,000 shares. If you own 1,000 of them, you own 0.1% of the company, and you would receive 0.1% of any dividend it pays out.

## Why it matters

- Over long periods, stocks have historically returned more than bonds or savings accounts, but with much larger ups and downs along the way, and past returns do not guarantee future ones.
- A share price reflects what investors expect the company to earn in the future, not only what it earns today.
- If a company goes bankrupt, shareholders are paid last, after lenders, and can lose everything they invested.
