## Definition

Deflation is a period during which the average price of goods and services goes down, so inflation is negative. It is different from **disinflation**, which only means that inflation is slowing down (prices still rise, just more slowly).

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If a consumer price index falls from 100 to 99 over a year, prices have dropped by 1% on average: that is deflation.

## Why it matters

- Falling prices can sound good, but persistent deflation is usually a sign of weak demand. People may delay purchases because they expect things to be cheaper later, which slows the economy further.
- Debts become heavier in real terms: the amount owed stays fixed while prices and often wages fall.
- Central banks fight deflation by cutting interest rates and, when rates are already close to zero, with tools such as quantitative easing. Japan is the best-known example of an economy that struggled with mild deflation for many years from the late 1990s.
