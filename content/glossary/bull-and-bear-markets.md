## Definition

- A **bull market** is a sustained period during which prices rise and investors are optimistic.
- A **bear market** is commonly defined as a fall of **20% or more** from a recent high, usually for a broad index such as the S&P 500.
- A **correction** is a smaller decline, commonly defined as 10% or more from a recent high.

These thresholds are conventions used by the financial press, not official rules. "Bullish" and "bearish" describe someone who expects prices to rise or fall.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* An index peaks at 5,000 points and then falls to 3,900. That is a 22% decline, so the index is said to be "in a bear market".

## Why it matters

- These labels help describe the general direction and mood of markets.
- Bear markets often, but not always, accompany recessions.
- No one can reliably predict when a bull market will end or a bear market will bottom out, which is why long-term investors focus on diversification rather than timing.
