## Definition

The yield curve shows the yields on bonds from the same issuer, usually a government, plotted by maturity: 3 months, 2 years, 5 years, 10 years, 30 years and so on.

Normally the curve **slopes upward**: lenders ask for a higher yield to tie up their money for longer, because more can go wrong over a long period. The shape changes as expectations for growth, inflation and central bank policy change:
- a **steeper** curve means the gap between long and short yields is widening;
- a **flatter** curve means the gap is shrinking.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If 2-year government bonds yield 3% and 10-year bonds yield 4%, the curve is upward sloping with a "2s10s" spread of 1 percentage point.

## Why it matters

- Short-term yields are closely tied to the central bank's policy rate; long-term yields reflect expectations for growth and inflation over many years.
- Banks borrow short and lend long, so the slope of the curve affects their profits and their willingness to lend.
- Changes in the curve's shape are read as signals about the economic outlook.
