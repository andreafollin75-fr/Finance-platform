## Definition

The European Central Bank, based in Frankfurt, is the central bank for the countries that use the euro (the euro area). Its main job, set by the EU treaties, is **price stability**. It defines this as inflation of 2% over the medium term, and treats deviations above and below that level as equally undesirable.

Monetary policy decisions are taken by the **Governing Council**, made up of the ECB's Executive Board and the governors of the national central banks of euro area countries. Its President chairs the meetings and explains the decisions at a press conference.

## Example

When inflation in the euro area rose sharply in 2022, the ECB raised its key interest rates several times to bring inflation back towards 2%. When inflation eased, it began to cut them.

## Why it matters

- ECB decisions affect borrowing costs for households, companies and governments in every euro area country.
- They also move the euro's exchange rate and European bond and stock markets.
- The ECB supervises the largest banks in the euro area, so it also plays a role in financial stability.
