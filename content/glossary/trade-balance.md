## Definition

The trade balance is the value of a country's exports minus the value of its imports, over a period.
- If exports are larger, the country has a **trade surplus**.
- If imports are larger, it has a **trade deficit**.

It is part of a wider measure, the **current account**, which also includes income from investments abroad and transfers.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A country exports goods and services worth €500 billion in a year and imports €450 billion. Its trade surplus is €50 billion.

## Why it matters

- Trade balances affect exchange rates and are often at the centre of political disputes and tariff decisions.
- A deficit is not necessarily bad: it can reflect strong domestic demand, or a country that attracts foreign investment.
- Large, persistent imbalances between big economies are watched as a possible source of tension and instability.
