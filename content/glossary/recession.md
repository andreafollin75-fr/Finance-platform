## Definition

A recession is a period when economic activity falls across the economy: output, incomes, employment, spending and industrial production all decline.

A widely used rule of thumb is **two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP**. But it is only a rule of thumb. In the United States, recessions are officially dated by a committee of the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER), which looks at many indicators, not just GDP. The CEPR plays a similar role for the euro area.

A **soft landing** is when a central bank manages to slow inflation without causing a recession; a **hard landing** is when the slowdown turns into one.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A country's real GDP shrinks by 0.3% in one quarter and by 0.5% in the next. Under the rule of thumb, it is in a "technical recession".

## Why it matters

- Recessions usually bring job losses, falling profits and lower tax revenues.
- Central banks tend to cut interest rates and governments often increase spending to support the economy.
- Recessions are usually only confirmed after they have started, because the data arrive with a delay.
