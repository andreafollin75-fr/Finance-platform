## Definition

Volatility measures how strongly the price of an asset swings over time. Statistically, it is usually the **standard deviation** of returns, expressed as an annual percentage.

- **Historical volatility** is calculated from past price moves.
- **Implied volatility** is the volatility that option prices suggest traders expect for the future.

High volatility means large and frequent price swings; low volatility means a calmer market.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* Two shares both end the year 5% higher. One moved by less than 1% on most days; the other often jumped or fell by 4% in a day. The second one was far more volatile, and therefore riskier to hold over short periods.

## Why it matters

- Volatility is the most common measure of investment risk.
- It tends to rise sharply during crises and falls when markets are calm and rising.
- It is a key input in the price of options: the more volatile the underlying asset, the more expensive its options.
