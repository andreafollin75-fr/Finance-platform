## Definition

Core inflation is a measure of price rises that leaves out items whose prices jump around a lot from month to month. In the United States it usually excludes **food and energy**. In the euro area, the commonly quoted core measure excludes **energy, food, alcohol and tobacco**.

The idea is to see the underlying trend in prices, without the noise caused by, for example, a sudden spike in oil prices.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If petrol prices surge because of a supply shock, headline inflation can jump in one month while core inflation barely moves. That tells economists the rise may be temporary rather than a broad increase in prices across the economy.

## Why it matters

- Central banks watch core inflation closely because it is a better guide to where inflation is heading over the next year or two.
- The Federal Reserve's 2% target is defined using the PCE price index (personal consumption expenditures), and it pays particular attention to core PCE.
- A surprise in core inflation often moves markets more than a surprise in headline inflation.
