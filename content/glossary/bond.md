## Definition

A bond is a debt security. When you buy a bond, you lend money to the issuer (a government, a company, a city...). In return, the issuer usually pays regular interest, called the **coupon**, and repays the original amount, called the **face value** or principal, on a fixed date called **maturity**.

Bonds can be bought and sold before maturity, so their market price moves every day.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A company issues a 5-year bond with a face value of €1,000 and a 4% annual coupon. The holder receives €40 per year for five years, then gets the €1,000 back at maturity, as long as the company does not default.

## Why it matters

- Bonds are the main way governments and large companies borrow money.
- Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions: when market rates rise, existing bonds with lower coupons become less attractive and their price falls.
- Many pension funds and savers hold bonds because they usually move less than stocks.
