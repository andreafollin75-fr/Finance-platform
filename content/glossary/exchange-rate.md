## Definition

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. Currency pairs are written with the **base currency** first: **EUR/USD = 1.10** means one euro costs 1.10 US dollars.

- If EUR/USD rises, the euro has **strengthened** (appreciated) against the dollar.
- If it falls, the euro has **weakened** (depreciated).

Most major currencies "float": their value is set every second by the foreign exchange ("forex" or "FX") market, the largest financial market in the world.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* EUR/USD moves from 1.10 to 1.05. A European tourist in New York now gets fewer dollars for each euro, so the trip costs more in euros; an American visiting Paris gets more euros per dollar.

## Why it matters

- Exchange rates affect the price of imports and exports, and therefore inflation and the profits of exporting companies.
- Interest rate differences between countries are a major driver: higher rates tend to attract capital and strengthen a currency.
- Many commodities, including oil, are priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes them more expensive for buyers in other currencies.
