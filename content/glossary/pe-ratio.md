## Definition

The price-to-earnings ratio compares a company's share price with its profit per share (earnings per share, or EPS):

**P/E = share price ÷ earnings per share**

It tells you how many years of current profits investors are paying for. A **trailing** P/E uses the profits of the last twelve months; a **forward** P/E uses analysts' forecasts for the next twelve months.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A share trades at $100 and the company earned $5 per share over the past year. Its P/E ratio is 100 ÷ 5 = 20.

## Why it matters

- A high P/E means investors expect strong profit growth, or are simply willing to pay a lot; a low P/E can mean a bargain, or a business in trouble.
- P/E ratios are most useful for comparing similar companies, or a company with its own history, rather than companies from very different sectors.
- It does not work for companies with no profits (the ratio is meaningless when earnings are zero or negative).
