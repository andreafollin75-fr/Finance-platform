## Definition

An option is a derivative contract. The buyer pays a price, the **premium**, to the seller in exchange for a right:
- a **call option** gives the right to **buy** the underlying asset at a fixed price (the **strike price**);
- a **put option** gives the right to **sell** it at the strike price.

The right lasts until the **expiry date**. If using the option would not be profitable, the buyer simply lets it expire and loses only the premium.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A share trades at $100. You pay $5 for a call option with a strike of $110 expiring in three months.
- If the share rises to $130, you can buy at $110: a gain of $20 per share, or $15 after the premium.
- If it stays below $110, the option expires worthless and you lose the $5.

## Why it matters

- Investors use put options as insurance against falling prices.
- Options let traders take large positions for a small premium, which makes them risky.
- Option prices reveal how much volatility traders expect, which is the basis of the VIX index.
