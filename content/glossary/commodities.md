## Definition

Commodities are basic goods that are interchangeable whatever their producer: a barrel of a given grade of crude oil is the same whoever pumped it. They are usually grouped into:
- **energy**: crude oil, natural gas, coal;
- **metals**: precious metals such as gold and silver, industrial metals such as copper and aluminium;
- **agriculture**: wheat, corn, soybeans, coffee, sugar...

Commodities are traded both physically and through **futures contracts** on exchanges.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A bakery chain buys wheat futures to lock in today's price for flour it will need in six months, protecting itself if the price of wheat rises.

## Why it matters

- Commodity prices feed directly into inflation, especially energy and food.
- They are sensitive to the global economy, weather, wars and political decisions.
- Some countries' economies and currencies depend heavily on commodity exports.
