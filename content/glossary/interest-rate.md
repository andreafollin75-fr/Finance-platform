## Definition

An interest rate is what a borrower pays a lender for the use of money, expressed as a percentage of the amount borrowed, usually per year. For a saver, it is the return earned for lending money to a bank (a deposit) or to a government or company (a bond).

A useful distinction:
- the **nominal** rate is the rate written on the contract;
- the **real** rate is the nominal rate minus inflation, which tells you how much purchasing power you actually gain or lose.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* You borrow €10,000 at 4% a year. After one year you owe €400 in interest. If inflation over that year is 3%, the real interest rate is about 1%.

## Why it matters

- Interest rates affect how much households pay for mortgages and how cheaply companies can finance investment.
- When rates rise, bonds that were issued earlier at lower rates lose value, and stocks can fall because future profits are worth less today.
- Central banks influence interest rates through their policy rate, but longer-term rates are also set by markets, based on expectations for inflation and growth.
