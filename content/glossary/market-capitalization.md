## Definition

Market capitalisation ("market cap") is the value the stock market places on a whole company:

**market cap = share price × number of shares outstanding**

Companies are often grouped by size: **large-cap**, **mid-cap** and **small-cap**. The exact thresholds vary between index providers and countries.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A company has 2 billion shares, and each trades at $50. Its market capitalisation is 2 billion × $50 = $100 billion.

## Why it matters

- Market cap is the usual way to compare the size of listed companies.
- Many indices, including the S&P 500 and the Euro Stoxx 50, give bigger weights to companies with a larger market cap, so a few giant companies can drive the whole index.
- It reflects the value of the shares only; it is not the same as what it would cost to buy the whole company, which also depends on its debt.
