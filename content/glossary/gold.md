## Definition

Gold is a precious metal that has been used as money and a store of value for thousands of years. Today it is held by central banks in their reserves, by investors (as bars, coins or through funds) and used in jewellery and industry. Its price is usually quoted in US dollars per **troy ounce** (about 31.1 grams).

Gold pays no interest or dividend. Its return comes only from changes in its price.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* During a sudden market panic, investors sell shares and buy gold, pushing its price up. This "safe haven" behaviour is common, though not guaranteed.

## Why it matters

- Gold is often seen as protection against inflation, currency weakness and crises.
- Because it pays no interest, it tends to become less attractive when real interest rates rise, and more attractive when they fall.
- Purchases by central banks are an important source of demand.
