## Definition

Diversification means not putting all your eggs in one basket. Instead of holding a single share or a single type of asset, an investor spreads money across:
- many companies;
- different sectors and countries;
- different asset types (shares, bonds, cash...).

It works best when the investments do not all move together: losses in some can be offset by gains, or smaller losses, in others.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* An investor who holds only one company's shares can lose a large part of their money if that company fails. An investor holding a fund of 500 companies would see only a small loss from the same failure.

## Why it matters

- Diversification reduces the risk specific to one company or sector, without necessarily reducing expected returns.
- It does not remove **market risk**: in a broad crash, most assets can fall together.
- Low-cost index funds and ETFs make diversification accessible to small investors.
