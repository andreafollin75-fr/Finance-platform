## Definition

A dividend is a payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually from its profits. The board of directors decides whether to pay one and how much. Many US companies pay dividends every quarter; many European companies pay once or twice a year.

The **dividend yield** is the annual dividend per share divided by the share price.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A share costs $50 and the company pays $2 of dividends per share over a year. The dividend yield is $2 ÷ $50 = 4%.

## Why it matters

- Dividends are a source of regular income for investors, alongside any rise in the share price.
- A company that cuts its dividend is often signalling financial difficulty, and its shares can fall sharply.
- Not paying a dividend is not necessarily bad: fast-growing companies often prefer to reinvest their profits, or to buy back their own shares instead.
