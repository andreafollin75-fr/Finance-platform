## Definition

Inflation is the rate at which the average price of goods and services rises over a period, usually measured over twelve months. It is most often tracked with a **consumer price index (CPI)**: statisticians follow the prices of a fixed "basket" of things households buy (food, rent, energy, transport, services...) and measure how the total cost of that basket changes.

When inflation is positive, money loses purchasing power: the same amount buys fewer things than before.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If the basket costs €100 in January one year and €103 in January the next year, annual inflation is 3%. Someone whose salary stayed the same can now buy about 3% less with it.

## Why it matters

- Central banks such as the ECB and the Federal Reserve aim for inflation of around 2% a year. When inflation runs above that target, they tend to raise interest rates; when it falls below, they tend to cut them.
- High inflation hurts savers and people on fixed incomes, and makes it harder for households and companies to plan.
- Inflation figures are among the most watched economic releases because they move expectations for interest rates, and therefore bond yields, currencies and stock prices.
