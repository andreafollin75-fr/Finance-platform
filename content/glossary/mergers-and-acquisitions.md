## Definition

- An **acquisition** (or takeover) is when one company buys control of another.
- A **merger** is when two companies combine into a single new company, often presented as a deal between equals.

Payment can be in cash, in the buyer's own shares, or a mix. A **hostile** takeover is one the target company's board does not support. Large deals often need approval from competition authorities, such as the European Commission or the US Federal Trade Commission and Department of Justice.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* Company A offers $60 per share for Company B, whose shares traded at $50 the day before. The extra $10 is a **takeover premium** of 20%, and B's share price usually jumps towards the offer price.

## Why it matters

- Deals can reshape whole industries and affect prices, jobs and competition.
- The buyer's share price often falls when a deal is announced, if investors think it is paying too much.
- The level of M&A activity is a gauge of business confidence and of how easy it is to borrow.
