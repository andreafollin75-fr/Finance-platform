## Definition

A credit rating is an opinion, given by a credit rating agency, on the ability of a borrower (a government or a company) to repay its debts in full and on time. The three largest agencies are **S&P Global Ratings, Moody's and Fitch**.

Ratings use letter scales. At S&P and Fitch the top is **AAA**, going down through AA, A, BBB, BB and so on; Moody's uses Aaa, Aa, A, Baa, Ba... Ratings of **BBB− (Baa3 at Moody's) and above** are called **investment grade**. Lower ratings are called **high yield** or, informally, "junk".

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If an agency **downgrades** a company from A to BBB, investors see its debt as somewhat riskier, and the company will probably have to pay a higher interest rate on its next bonds.

## Why it matters

- Ratings influence borrowing costs: a lower rating generally means higher interest rates.
- Many funds are only allowed to hold investment-grade bonds, so a downgrade below that line can force them to sell.
- Ratings are opinions, not guarantees: agencies were strongly criticised for rating risky mortgage securities too highly before the 2008 financial crisis.
