## Definition

Gross domestic product measures the value of everything produced within a country's borders over a period, usually a quarter or a year. It can be calculated by adding up spending (consumption + investment + government spending + exports − imports).

Two versions are important:
- **nominal GDP** uses current prices;
- **real GDP** removes the effect of inflation, so it shows whether the economy is really producing more.

**GDP growth** is the percentage change in real GDP. Countries report it differently: the US usually quotes an annualised quarterly rate, while the euro area usually quotes the change from the previous quarter.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If nominal GDP rises by 5% in a year but prices rise by 3%, real GDP has grown by about 2%.

## Why it matters

- GDP growth is the headline measure of how well an economy is doing.
- Debt and deficits are usually expressed as a percentage of GDP, to compare countries of different sizes.
- First GDP estimates are often revised later, sometimes significantly.
