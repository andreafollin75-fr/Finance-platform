## Definition

The unemployment rate is the number of unemployed people divided by the **labour force** (the employed plus the unemployed). Under the international (ILO) definition, a person is unemployed if they have no job, are available to work, and have actively looked for work recently.

People who are not looking for work, such as many students and retirees, are not counted in the labour force at all.

In the United States the monthly **jobs report** from the Bureau of Labor Statistics gives both the unemployment rate and **nonfarm payrolls**, the number of jobs added or lost.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A country has a labour force of 30 million people, of whom 1.5 million are unemployed. The unemployment rate is 1.5 ÷ 30 = 5%.

## Why it matters

- It is one of the clearest signs of the health of an economy and of household incomes.
- The Federal Reserve's mandate explicitly includes maximum employment.
- A very tight labour market can push wages, and then inflation, higher.
