## Definition

Public debt is the total stock of money a government owes to its lenders: holders of its bonds, banks and other creditors. It grows each year the government runs a budget deficit and borrows to cover it.

To compare countries of different sizes, debt is usually expressed as a **percentage of GDP** (the "debt-to-GDP ratio"). EU rules set a reference value of 60% of GDP, although many member states are above it.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A country owes €2 trillion and its yearly GDP is €2.5 trillion. Its debt-to-GDP ratio is 2 ÷ 2.5 = 80%.

## Why it matters

- The more a government owes, the more it must spend on interest, especially when interest rates rise.
- High debt can lead investors to demand higher yields, and can trigger credit rating downgrades.
- What matters is not only the level of debt but also its cost, how quickly the economy is growing, and how confident lenders are.
