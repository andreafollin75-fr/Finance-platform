## Definition

Government bonds are loans that investors make to a national government. Each country's bonds have a familiar name:
- **Treasuries**: United States (bills, notes and bonds depending on maturity);
- **Bunds**: Germany;
- **OATs** (Obligations assimilables du Trésor): France;
- **BTPs**: Italy;
- **gilts**: United Kingdom.

Bonds of governments considered very safe are used as the "risk-free" reference against which other borrowers are compared.

## Example

The 10-year US Treasury yield and the 10-year German Bund yield are the reference long-term interest rates for the United States and the euro area. News articles often say "Treasury yields rose", meaning the market interest rate on US government debt went up.

## Why it matters

- Government bond yields influence mortgage rates and the borrowing costs of companies.
- The gap (spread) between, for example, French OATs and German Bunds shows how much more investors charge to lend to France than to Germany.
- In times of stress, investors often buy government bonds of safe countries, a move called a "flight to safety".
