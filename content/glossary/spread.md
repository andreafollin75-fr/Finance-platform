## Definition

In finance, a spread is simply the gap between two numbers. The most common uses are:
- **Yield spread**: the difference between the yields of two bonds, for example Italian versus German 10-year government bonds.
- **Credit spread**: the extra yield a company bond pays over a government bond of similar maturity, as compensation for the risk that the company might not repay.
- **Bid-ask spread**: the gap between the price buyers offer and the price sellers ask for an asset.

Yield spreads are usually quoted in basis points.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If a 10-year Italian bond yields 3.6% and a 10-year German Bund yields 2.4%, the spread is 1.2 percentage points, or 120 basis points.

## Why it matters

- Widening spreads mean investors want more compensation for risk: they often signal worry about a country, a company or the economy.
- Narrowing spreads usually signal more confidence.
- In the euro area, spreads between countries' bonds are watched closely as a gauge of stress, as during the euro debt crisis of 2010–2012.
