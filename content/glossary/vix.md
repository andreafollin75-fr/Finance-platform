## Definition

The VIX is calculated by Cboe (the Chicago Board Options Exchange) from the prices of options on the S&P 500 index. It expresses the market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the coming 30 days, as an annualised percentage.

It does not predict the direction of the market. It measures how big traders expect moves to be, in either direction.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* A VIX reading of 15 suggests traders expect relatively calm US markets, while a reading of 40 would signal that they expect large swings, as typically seen during market panics.

## Why it matters

- The VIX usually jumps when stocks fall sharply, which is why it is nicknamed the "fear gauge".
- Investors and journalists use it as a quick read of market stress.
- A low VIX is not a guarantee of calm: it describes expectations, which can change very quickly.
