## Definition

The word has two related meanings:
- **Market liquidity**: how easily an asset can be traded quickly, in large amounts, at a price close to its current value. Large-company shares and major government bonds are very liquid; a house or a piece of art is not.
- **Funding liquidity**: how easily banks, companies and investors can get cash when they need it, for example by borrowing.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* An investor needs to sell a large holding of a little-traded small-company share. There are few buyers, so to sell quickly the investor has to accept a price well below the last trade. That share is illiquid.

## Why it matters

- In a crisis, liquidity can vanish suddenly, forcing sales at very low prices and spreading losses.
- Banks can fail because they run out of cash even when their assets would be worth enough over time, as happened in several bank runs.
- Central banks act as "lenders of last resort", providing liquidity to keep the financial system working.
