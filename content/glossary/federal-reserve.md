## Definition

The Federal Reserve System, usually called "the Fed", is the central bank of the United States, created in 1913. Congress has given it a **dual mandate**: maximum employment and stable prices. It aims for inflation of 2% over the longer run, measured with the PCE price index.

Interest rate decisions are taken by the **Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC)**, which normally meets eight times a year. It sets a target range for the **federal funds rate**, the rate at which banks lend to each other overnight.

## Example

After each meeting the FOMC publishes a statement, and the Fed Chair holds a press conference. Four times a year the committee also publishes members' projections for growth, unemployment, inflation and rates, including the "dot plot" of where each member expects rates to be.

## Why it matters

- Because the US dollar is the world's main reserve currency, Fed decisions affect markets everywhere, not only in the US.
- Changes in the federal funds rate feed through to US mortgages, loans and Treasury yields.
- The Fed's words about the future ("forward guidance") can move markets as much as its actual decisions.
