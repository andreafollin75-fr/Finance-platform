## Definition

Short selling is a way to bet that a price will fall:
1. the investor borrows shares, usually from a broker, and pays a fee to do so;
2. sells them immediately at today's price;
3. later buys the same number of shares back and returns them to the lender.

If the price has fallen in the meantime, the investor keeps the difference as profit. If it has risen, they lose money.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* An investor borrows and sells 100 shares at $50 ($5,000). The price falls to $40; they buy 100 shares back for $4,000 and return them. Profit: $1,000, minus fees. If instead the price had risen to $70, buying back would cost $7,000: a loss of $2,000.

## Why it matters

- The potential loss is in theory unlimited, because there is no ceiling on how high a price can rise.
- Short sellers can help markets by exposing overvalued or fraudulent companies.
- When a heavily shorted share rises sharply, short sellers may rush to buy it back, pushing the price up even more: a **short squeeze**.
- Regulators sometimes restrict short selling during periods of market stress.
