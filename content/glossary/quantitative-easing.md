## Definition

Quantitative easing is when a central bank creates new central bank money (reserves) and uses it to buy financial assets, mainly government bonds, on a large scale.

This pushes up bond prices and therefore pushes down long-term yields. Lower long-term rates make borrowing cheaper for households, companies and governments, and encourage investors to move into riskier assets.

Central banks usually turn to QE when their policy rate is already close to zero and they want to provide more support.

## Example

After the 2008 financial crisis, the Federal Reserve and the Bank of England launched large bond-buying programmes. The ECB started a broad programme of government bond purchases in 2015, when inflation in the euro area was very low.

## Why it matters

- QE is a powerful tool to fight financial crises and the risk of deflation.
- It makes central banks very large holders of government debt.
- Critics argue it can inflate asset prices, such as stocks and housing, and benefit those who already own them.
