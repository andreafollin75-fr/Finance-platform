## Definition

An exchange-traded fund is a fund that holds a basket of assets (shares, bonds, commodities...) and whose own units are bought and sold on a stock exchange during the trading day, just like a share.

Most ETFs are **passive**: they aim to replicate the performance of an index, such as the S&P 500 or the Euro Stoxx 50, rather than trying to beat it. Their annual fees are typically low compared with actively managed funds.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* Instead of buying shares of 500 companies one by one, an investor buys a single S&P 500 ETF. If the index rises 10% over a year, the ETF should rise by roughly 10%, minus its small fees.

## Why it matters

- ETFs make diversification easy and cheap, even with small amounts of money.
- They have become one of the most popular ways for individuals to invest.
- An ETF is only as diversified as what it holds: a fund focused on one sector or one country can still be very risky.
