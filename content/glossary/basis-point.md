## Definition

A basis point (often written **bp** or **bps**) is 0.01 percentage point. 100 basis points equal 1 percentage point.

Professionals use basis points to avoid confusion. Saying a rate "rose by 1%" is ambiguous: did it go from 4% to 5% (one percentage point) or from 4% to 4.04% (one percent of 4%)? "Rose by 100 basis points" can only mean 4% to 5%.

## Example

*Hypothetical example.* If a central bank moves its policy rate from 3.00% to 3.25%, it has raised it by 25 basis points. If a 10-year bond yield goes from 2.50% to 2.43%, it has fallen by 7 basis points.

## Why it matters

- Central bank decisions, bond yields and spreads are almost always reported in basis points in the financial press.
- Small moves matter: on large loans or bond portfolios, a few basis points can represent a lot of money.
