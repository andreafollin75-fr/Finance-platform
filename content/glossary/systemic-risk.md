## Definition

Systemic risk is the danger that a problem in one part of the financial system, such as the collapse of a big bank, spreads to others and causes a wider crisis that hurts the whole economy.

It spreads through links between institutions (they lend to each other and trade with each other), through forced sales that push down prices for everyone, and through loss of confidence, such as **bank runs**, where many depositors try to withdraw their money at once.

Banks considered so important that their failure would threaten the system are described as **systemically important**, or "too big to fail".

## Example

The bankruptcy of the US investment bank Lehman Brothers in September 2008 caused panic across global markets, froze lending between banks and turned a US housing crisis into a worldwide financial crisis.

## Why it matters

- Systemic crises are very costly: they can cause deep recessions and require huge public support.
- Since 2008, regulators have required large banks to hold more capital and liquidity, and run regular **stress tests**.
- Central banks publish financial stability reports that assess these risks.
